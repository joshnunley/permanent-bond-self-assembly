# The finite check of late first nonpath bodies

The [all-side-count argument](all-side-counts.md) needs a finite assertion independent of the side count. If the first nonpath has size 6 through 14, the prefix cannot reach stock 15. This page explains the reduction used by the [exact checker](../code/late_cycle_extensions.py). Angles are measured in turns and $e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)$.

At the first nonpath size $m$, the split $1+(m-1)$ gives an old path and a new vertex. For each candidate adjacency set of the new cell, the part of any split avoiding that vertex is an interval of the old path, and the complementary induced graph must be a path. The graph enumeration tries all such intervals. For $m\ge8$ its surviving cases have two neighbors of the new cell at old path distance at least three. The chain-word enumeration gives the possible signed turn words before $m$. These reductions use necessary conditions and can retain additional cases.

## Enumerating short closures

**Short-closure lemma.** Suppose the phase of each term in a sum of at most ten roots of unity is an affine integer function of free angles, with rational constant term, modulo one. The torsion zeros of the sum can be covered by finitely many explicitly constructed torsion cosets on which the sum vanishes identically.

*Proof.* Partition a vanishing sum into nonempty minimal vanishing subsums. Table 1 of Poonen and Rubinstein gives the regular prime sums for primes 2, 3, 5 and 7, and the listed modifications of the sums for 5 and 7. One modification replaces distinct terms $z$ by pairs $z e(1/6),z e(-1/6)$. For 5 there are one through four replacements and for 7 one through three. Enumerate every choice of replaced terms. The other type at weight ten replaces one term $z$ of the regular seven-term sum by the four terms $-z e(j/5)$ for $1\le j\le4$. Both replacements preserve the sum of the terms they replace. [Poonen and Rubinstein, Table 1 and Theorem 3](https://klein.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/ngon.pdf).

Choose the block containing the first term. For each template of the right size, choose which template term is first, and match the other terms to those of the block. Equate their phases relative to the first and repeat with the remaining terms. These equations are linear congruences. On a coset $q+B s$, a new equation has the form $uB s=b-uq\pmod 1$. If $uB\ne0$, an integer unimodular change of coordinates makes $uB=(g,0,\ldots,0)$ with $g>0$ the gcd. The first coordinate then takes one of $g$ rational values modulo one, with the other coordinates still free. A zero row gives a constant condition. Thus every step is exact and preserves rational parametrizations.

Any torsion zero yields one of the block choices. Each resulting coset has a partition into blocks that cancel for all its parameters. The implementation can first remove entire zero Laurent coefficients. It can also accept or reject immediately if the remaining sum is identically zero or a single nonzero Laurent monomial. These operations preserve the needed cover. ∎

## The first body and the next splits

Take the chain prefixes from the [chain-word model](all-side-counts.md#odd-side-counts). A row through size 5 continues as a row by the [row lemma](continuation.md#the-row), so the old chain has at least one turn size. The word enumeration gives at most one turn size for the old chain sizes 10 through 13. Thus a first non-chain body with multiple old turn sizes has at most ten cells in the range being checked.

Write the first non-chain body as the old chain $Y$ plus $x$. Its possible graphs are the old path with $x$ joined to a nonempty subset of at most six old vertices. For any split into two smaller bodies, the part avoiding $x$ is an interval of $Y$. Check that the complement induces a path. Compare the two turn words with the required smaller words. For this stage reflections may be chosen independently in the two parts, so each split gives a finite disjunction of necessary affine equations obtained by subtracting consecutive edge headings.

For first non-chain sizes 6 and 7, and whenever there are multiple turn sizes, impose all those equations. A pair of consecutive old path vertices touching the new cell gives an equilateral triangle, so its two new edge headings come from the old edge heading by adding the appropriate sixth-turns. If the old vertices are two steps apart, their other common neighbour is the opposite corner of the unit parallelogram, giving both new headings directly. The calculation retains the remaining new headings as free angles wherever the split equations do not determine them.

For successive neighbours $i<j$ of the new cell, require $c_j-c_i=e(h_j)-e(h_i)$. The left side is the sum of the old steps from $i$ to $j$. The resulting sum of unit roots has at most $N_0$ terms. The short-closure lemma imposes every such identity on the angle cosets. With all these identities, the new cell is determined as $c_i-e(h_i)$ from any one neighbour and all prescribed contacts hold identically. This supplies the first-body data used by `late_cycles.py`. All cell coordinates and prescribed headings are Laurent sums or units in the free parameters.

For a single turn size at $N_0\ge8$, use the split $4+(N_0-4)$ directly. The chain containing the new cell $x$ has at least four cells, so an edge incident to $x$ is consecutive on that chain with an old edge. For each possible split and each allowed orientation of that chain, read the heading $x\to c_i$ from the turn at their common vertex. If $s$ represents the old turn, this heading is a unit of the form $\pm s^a$. The position of $x$ is then a Laurent sum in $s$. Each guessed contact $c_j$ imposes the Laurent equation $|c_j-x|^2-1=0$ on the unit circle. At least one is a nonzero polynomial. Indeed, at $s=1$ the old chain straightens into points at unit spacing, and two of the required neighbours have old indices at least three apart by the [contact-graph enumeration](../code/first_nonchain_graphs.py). They cannot both be unit distance from $x(1)$. Enumerate the torsion roots of a nonzero equation by the bound on the degree of the cyclotomic factor, and retain only those roots satisfying every required unit distance. These fixed points enter the same final extension check as the short-loop cases.

For a two-angle case, the code first rejects any repeated centre or constant distance below one, and any formal extra contact in a body required to be a chain. The remaining two-angle cases at sizes 6 and 7 are refined by one-cell extension in `late_cycle_next.py`. For these refinements, choose the case generated from the actual contact graph. Its prescribed contacts all hold identically, and its other formal contacts necessarily hold at the actual point. Thus the old body's formal contact graph equals its actual graph in this case. In the next body, guess the set of neighbours of the new cell and require every split whose two parts are smaller than $N_0$ to be a split into the two prescribed chains. Enumerate both induced paths, and impose the turn comparisons for both. For closure, use any shortest path in the old contact graph between consecutive neighbours of the new cell, in place of the old chain segment. This still gives at most as many terms in each equation as there are cells in the new body. The refinement is used only to reach sizes 7 and 8, so the short-closure lemma applies. It again supplies cases with all prescribed contacts holding identically and thus includes the actual contact graph in at least one case. This refinement allows reflections independently in each part. The full handed merge condition is imposed in the final searches.

## Verifying the extensions

At a fixed angle point the code builds the prescribed bodies in the cyclotomic field containing their phases and searches all compatible hand choices and subsequent one-cell extensions. All incidences, body identifications and placements are exact as in the [fixed-side-count arithmetic](fixed-side-count.md#arithmetic-and-completeness). Norm comparisons use the interval branch of `CycField` for every non-unit difference. There is no floating-point screening.

The one-angle check first runs a formal search on the whole circle. It retains every variable distance for overlap purposes and rejects only exact coincidences or constant distances less than one. When a contact is not formal, it records its possible unit-length equation. When a lookup fails, it records the possible equalities involving the first missing cell. If the three-cell part loses its middle cell and its two remaining cells supply a non-formal rotation, it records the possible equality of the two lengths. The search then checks every torsion zero of these logged polynomials in the fixed-angle search. This covers all torsion specializations by induction on the extension search. If a specialized step is absent from the formal tree, take the first such step. A new contact, placement rotation or lookup equality used by that step then vanishes at the given specialization. It is among the logged equations for the preceding formal body, and its torsion roots are searched separately. Logging the first missing cell suffices because a valid placement needs every missing cell to match some target. A one-term Laurent sum has constant modulus and can never acquire a new contact or coincidence under specialization. The root enumeration uses the bound on the degree of the cyclotomic factor and exact cyclotomic division. A modular evaluation at a primitive root in a prime field only rejects a candidate when nonzero, since a polynomial divisible by the cyclotomic polynomial would vanish at that root.

A ratio log whose length polynomial vanishes identically can be discarded. The denominator is a difference of two formal units. If it has two distinct exponents, all its nonzero roots in the parameter lie on the unit circle. Equality of the products with their reciprocal conjugates forces the numerator to have the same roots with the same multiplicities, and no other nonzero roots. If the denominator is a monomial, the numerator is a monomial by the same product identity. In both cases the ratio is $c\,s^j$ with $c$ in the coefficient field and $|c|=1$. At an admissible torsion placement the ratio is a root of unity, so $c$ is a root of unity in the coefficient field. Such a ratio is already formal and would not have been logged.

The final candidate generator uses the split $4+(N-4)$ for $N\ge8$. Every shape with at least four cells contains the four-cell chain. After removing any single cell from such a shape there is still a contact between two of the chain's cells. Hence the part containing the new cell can always be placed by anchoring an ordered touching pair among the old cells. For $N=7$ use $3+4$ and allow the two-anchor rotation of the preceding paragraph for the three-cell part. This is the finite branching argument with the chosen splits. The exact search and the formal search both require the handed split condition for [merge-closed families](geometry.md#definitions) at all sizes, including the prescribed starting bodies.

At size 6, twelve two-angle cases pass the initial geometry test. Four two-angle extensions to size 7 require another refinement to size 8, which leaves only points and one-angle cases. At size 7, sixteen two-angle cases pass the initial test. The check finds no extension past size 7 among those sixteen cases. At size 8 all two-angle cases fail the initial test. Every formal one-angle search stops before its cutoff. Every candidate point and every torsion specialization of the logged relations was checked with the full handed merge condition.

The checker also classifies the survivors at stock 14. None has first non-chain size 6 through 13. In the first-size-14 run, the four surviving point cases all have the shapes of consecutive arcs through the closed regular fourteen-cell ring. The check compares the entire prefix with the canonical shapes of the arcs at the exact turn $2\pi/14$.

**Reproduction.** From the repository root, run `python3 -B code/late_cycle_extensions.py N0` for each `N0` from 6 through 14. Every run uses the same cutoff of 15. The combined output is [the recorded output](../results/late_cycle_extensions.txt). As a cross-check, the rotation-class counts of the minimal-sum templates at weights 2–10 are $(1,1,0,1,1,3,3,4,6)$, agreeing with the source table. The direct exact octagon search `python3 -B code/exact_n.py 8 14` also reproduced the 21 listed prefixes.
